What grows after rice?

A field-level rotation check for four rice landscapes, built with NASA POWER daily rainfall.

The decision. Between rice harvest and the next transplant, a winter crop has one narrow window. A late harvest, a wet operation day or an unknown field can turn a good rotation into a missed rice season.

What we built

An interactive four-region explorer: pick a landscape, replay the crop, compare winter rotations and test the same plan across three NASA POWER seasons. Each status separates calendar fit, field conditions and operation-day rain; the sidebar close-up follows the crop and stage.

NASA data

NASA POWER Daily: corrected precipitation and minimum temperature, local solar time, 2021/22, 2023/24 and 2024/25 — 4 sites × 940 days = 3,760 records. NASA Blue Marble supplies the globe texture. A Copernicus Sentinel-2A image provides local context (not NASA data).

Try it in 30 seconds

  1. Kaiping → winter potato, 2024/25.
  2. Try a wet harvest → 39.34 mm / 3 days, 11 March 2024.
  3. Switch the season to 2024/25 → 0.59 mm at the corresponding operation.
  4. Open the three-season check → 2023/24 is wet; move the late-rice harvest to the latest date → calendar conflicts.

Method and limits

Local crop calendars come from cited extension and research sources. The planner uses a three-day rain window around each operation, an illustrative 30 mm review threshold and fixed crop durations and stages. Weather is a regional grid estimate, not a field sensor; drainage and soil texture are user inputs. Check local variety, maturity, pumps, field access and markets.

Validation

node scripts/check-model.cjs covers rotation rules, the 39.34 → 0.59 mm wet-harvest comparison, 940-day coverage for every site and season, and stage order. The interface was checked at 320, 390 and 1440 px in English and Chinese, across all four sites, with no console errors or horizontal page overflow. The app runs from file:// with its bundled data.
